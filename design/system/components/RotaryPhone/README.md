# RotaryPhone

The tablet softphone's phone: a 1960s desk rotary drawn in Treeline's hand (ink line over watercolor shading), three-dimensional, with a finger wheel that really turns and a handset that really lifts.

**Consumer provides:** `ringing` (the handset trembles), `number` (the treehouse's own 4 digits, shown in the middle of the dial), `colorway` (`berry`, a soft red, by default; `moss`, `ivory`), `onDigit` (each digit, once the dial has returned, only while the handset is lifted), `onHook` (lifted or hung up), `onPulse` (one call per pulse as the dial returns, for click sounds), optional `offHook`/`defaultOffHook`, `notice` (default on), `online` (the little light, default on), `apiRef` (`lift()`, `hangUp()`, `dial(n)`, `isOffHook()`, `setRinging(on)`, `setLine(on)`, `isLineOn()`). Plain HTML: `data-tl-rotary` on the wrapper and `js/treeline.js`; listen for `tl-rotary-digit`, `tl-rotary-hook` and `tl-rotary-pulse` on it.

- **Drawn after the Western Electric 500:** a rounded body with soft shoulders and sides that swell toward the base, a big dial with the digits printed on the body around it, a clear finger wheel with a dot at the back of each hole, the line's number on the center card, two cradle posts, and the classic one-piece handset: a handle that arches between two bell-shaped caps resting on the body's shoulders.
- **Parts that move** are separate layers in one drawing, all sharing one viewBox: the deck, the cord, the handset (lifts), the body and number ring, the card behind the wheel (stays still), the finger wheel (turns) and the glare and finger stop (stay still). The standalone files in `assets/Illustrations/rotary/` are the same layers in fixed colors.
- **Dialing works like the real thing:** put a finger in a hole, drag it clockwise to the finger stop and let go. The wheel only goes one way and stops at the stop. If you let go early, it returns and nothing is dialed. 1 turns 60 degrees and 0 turns 330, as on the real dial. It returns at a real phone's pace, about ten pulses a second, so 0 takes just over a second; `onPulse` fires once per pulse.
- **Lift and hang up** by tapping the handset; it tips open from the mouthpiece end, the earpiece rising high, and the coiled cord follows. A real button sits over the handset for keyboards and screen readers. Number keys dial when the phone has focus.
- **Ringing:** set `ringing` (or `api.setRinging(true)`). Only the handset trembles on its cradle, a hair's width, in bursts like the bell: about a second of shaking, then a rest. Nothing else moves, and there's no glow. It stops when the handset lifts. With reduced motion it doesn't move at all (the bell still rings).
- **The little light** at the front right of the base: green while the phone is connected (`online`, or `api.setLine(true)`; plain HTML `data-line="on"` on the wrapper), dark when it isn't. While it rings it blinks softly with the bell. With reduced motion it stays lit instead of blinking. `api.isLineOn()` reads it.
- Digits only count while the handset is lifted, as on a real phone. Collect four, then call.
- **Always shows "Can't call 911 or outside numbers"** under the phone. Turn `notice` off only where the same screen already says it.
- Colors come from `--tlp-l`, `--tlp-m`, `--tlp-d`, `--tlp-s` on the wrapper. The phone keeps its own colors in Lantern; it's an object on the page, not part of the UI chrome.
- **The softphone screen is `SoftphoneDesk`:** this phone on a side table in a 90s room, with the paper props around it standing in for the footer and help. Use `RotaryPhone` on its own only where the room doesn't fit, with its notice and the small footer. No number pad, no caller ID, no screen of digits.
- Each phone on a page needs its own `id` (React does this for you; in plain HTML give each copy's `id="tlp-…"` and `url(#tlp-…)` references their own prefix).
- Motion respects reduced-motion settings: the wheel and handset jump instead of animating.
