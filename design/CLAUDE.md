# Treeline design system: notes for Claude Code

This folder is the Treeline design system. Build every parent-app screen from it; don't invent new styles.

- `ui/` is the plain-HTML kit: `css/tokens.css` + `css/treeline.css` (self-hosted fonts in `fonts/`), `js/treeline.js` (menus, sheets, toasts, tabs, code boxes, sliders, password toggle), `components/*.html` (exact markup for every variant) and `components/*.md` (the rules for each). `ui/app.html` is a working screen that uses the shell and all four page templates. Open `ui/index.html` to see everything.
- `system/` is the source: `README.md` (brand book: color, type, watercolor, logo and illustration rules), `Layout.md` (spacing, sizing, app layout, phone vs desktop for every component), `tokens.json`, `components/bundle.js` (React versions on `window.Treeline`, typed in `components/index.d.ts`) and `assets/` (logos, exports, illustrations, icons).

## Rules to follow when writing UI

1. Copy markup from `ui/components/<Name>.html` and keep its classes and nesting. Use tokens (`var(--space-4)`, `var(--ink)`), never raw values.
2. Every screen sits in `.tl-app` (the shell: `.tl-app > .tl-app__layout > header.tl-appbar, main.tl-app__main, nav.tl-nav`) or has `class="tl-root"` on `<body>`. Components use container queries, so without one the phone layout never applies.
3. Sheets and the `.tl-toasts` region are the last children of `.tl-app`, never inside main. No `backdrop-filter`, `transform` or `filter` on the app bar.
4. Navigation is always Home, Strings, Calls, Quiet hours, Settings. Settings is the treehouse's own page (details, parent code, notifications, records, leaving); the signed-in person's profile is the one page behind the avatar. Home opens with the treehouse, and right under it three small buttons: "New string connection" (the same sheet as on Strings), "Phone book" (the printable phone book, which is no longer in the nav) and "Pause all calls" (the `pause` icon; while paused it reads "Resume calls" with the `play` icon). The header bar holds only the logo, the treehouse's name and the account menu. Add a phone offers four choices: a softphone, a phone adapter, the Treeline rotary phone, and "This device" (the browser in hand becomes a phone, no code to type). A family's string code is also a way into the closed beta for someone new: they sign in, enter it as their invite, set up their treehouse, and the string request is sent for them. One `h1` per screen, in the app bar. The Strings page shows the Grove map (Map | List); one "New string connection" button (the `string` icon) opens one sheet with a QR code, your code and a place to type theirs. Each screen is one template: Overview, List and detail, Settings form or Focused task (a sheet).
5. No light-green backgrounds; green (`canopy`) is for accents and the primary button only. A few toned cards per screen (at most a third of the cards, never three in a row). Every phone icon is the rotary phone.
6. **Actions: one row, left-aligned, primary first.** Buttons at the end of a card, form, sheet or dialog (`.tl-actions`) sit side by side, aligned with each other and left-aligned as a group, at every width; write the primary last in the markup and it shows first. Never right-aligned, never stacked as full-width bars. The button beside the primary is `secondary` (the default), not `quiet`.
7. Test at 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1440 in both themes (`data-theme="light"` / `"dark"` on `<html>`): nothing may scroll sideways, and the app bar title lines up with the first card.
8. **No 911, said plainly.** The softphone says it with the `RotaryPhone`'s own notice under the phone (a small pill with a berry warning triangle: "Can’t call 911 or outside numbers"), always there and never turned off or replaced with an `Alert`; the How to dial guide has a step for it too. The parent app says it during setup (the parent ticks "I understand"), on Home, and in About. Never hide it in settings.
9. **Footer in every app** (`footer()` / `footerLinks()` in `web/shared/ui.js`, `.tl-footer`): a small line at the bottom in `ink-muted`: "Made by Jordan Miller · © 2026", "☕ Buy me a coffee" (the generic `coffee` icon, not Buy Me a Coffee's logo, which has no published usage terms; links to `https://buymeacoffee.com/jdmiller`), "✉ Email Jordan" (the `mail` icon; `mailto:jdmiller2010@gmail.com`) and "Open-source licenses" (a plain page built from `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, with Treeline's own licenses first).
10. **The softphone screen is an ordinary Treeline page**, not a room: the brand mark and name with the tagline under it (not a link) and a quiet-time `Tag` in the header, then the berry `RotaryPhone` as big as the screen allows (the dial is what a finger has to hit: the picture's empty margin may run past the screen edge, its empty top slides up under the header, and the dial must never sit below the fold) on a soft `wash-sun` glow. The treehouse's name (the one `h1`) and what the phone is doing (a `Presence` and a live status line) are not drawn: they stay in the page, visually hidden, for screen readers. In one column the `CardFile` of approved strings sits under the phone, then the phone's own no-911 notice, then one secondary "How to dial" button (centred on tablets) that opens a `Sheet` of the guide's nine pictures (`design/ui/illustrations/guide/`) with compact `Steps`, Back and Next; then the footer. On landscape screens of 900px and up the card file, the no-911 notice stays under the phone, and "How to dial" and the card file stack in a left column beside the phone. Tapping the phone never flashes a highlight or selects text, and a device that redraws slowly drops the painted texture. Pass the treehouse's `number`; fill the card file with the strings. Listen for `tl-rotary-digit` (four digits make a call), `tl-rotary-hook` (lift answers or starts dialing, hang-up ends the call) and `tl-rotary-pulse` (play a click). Don't add a number pad, caller ID or a display of digits. (`SoftphoneDesk`, the room, stays in the design system but the app doesn't use it.)
11. **The artist's layer: doodles and scenes, and the system comes first.** The UI is ordinary; over it an artist draws small pencil-and-wash doodles as if on paper (design system README, "The artist's layer"). They obey physics (grounded on a surface with a shadow, in the air with a faint shadow far below, or clinging by one end), and a few rules never bend: never over text or a control, take no room or scroll (a doodle stands only on the top edge of a card that has a `.tl-stack--sections` gap above it, read from the markup, in an absolutely placed `.tl-doodle-spot`; every doodle fits that 48px gap, so nothing is measured; the smoke test proves no element moves with them on or off; never add space for one), inert (`aria-hidden`, no pointer events, hidden in print), a sprinkle, never every spot that could take one (usually one or two on a phone, two or three on a wide screen, sometimes fewer), still under reduced motion, never shrunk to fit. They are placed at random per page and per day (`TLDoodles.scatter`): the same all day, different tomorrow, and a card can carry a `data-scene` for a small arrangement that belongs to it (acorn, forest, fern, sprout, bloom), at most one or two a page. No doodles on forms being filled in, safety or error copy, or the phone screen. Otherwise nature touches stay in the components that already carry them (`Steps`, `LeafDivider`, the leaf `Toast` and `Tag`, `Card` `sprig`). To add or change anything: write it in `design/system/` and its README first, then the `ui/` copy, then use it.
12. **One art direction, three scales.** Plates (illustrations), painted cards (toned cards: breeze, blossom, oak, fern, seeds, maple) and marginalia (the artist's layer) are one hand: pencil line then wash, tokens-only pigments, light from the top-right, things attached to the world, botanical not mascot, art never covers or moves the UI, less is the style. A few painted cards per view (at most a third of the cards, never three in a row); the artist's layer stays off toned cards and is a sparse sprinkle (often none). New art is added to the README's "Art direction" tables first, then built. See the design system README, "Art direction: one hand, three scales".
13. **The front door and setup.** Sign-in is the logo, a one-line `h1` (the tagline, "Safe, simple calls."), the `Welcome` (four small plates on white shapes: your treehouse, a string to another treehouse, kids pick up and dial, just a phone call: no texts, voicemail, caller ID or group calls, never recorded), one card titled "Log in to Treeline" with the `GoogleButton` (Google's own button, the page's one main action) and the secondary "Use as a phone" beside it (a `Sheet` with a pairing QR code), a "Closed beta" tag beside the title and the no-911 line at the foot of the card. On a 390px phone all of it fits on one screen without scrolling (the smoke test measures it). A new person signs in with Google first and then sees "Enter your invite code"; a wrong code turns the field red (`has-error`), like a wrong password. Setup is two steps (name your treehouse, then the 911 notice and consent with both boxes at the bottom); the time zone comes from the device. A first phone and a first string are a notice at the top of Home, not setup steps.
